## Coordinate system

All output coordinates are defined in the coordinate system of `current_state`.

* The origin is the top-left image corner.
* x increases from left to right.
* y increases from top to bottom.
* Normalize x and y independently to [0, 1].
* x = pixel_x / (image_width - 1)
* y = pixel_y / (image_height - 1)

Do not predict any background transformation.

## How to draw pseudo_preview_semantic_overlay

### 1. Yellow polygons

Semi-transparent yellow masked regions representing the sketch support plane.

Represent every disconnected visible yellow region as a separate closed polygon.

### 2. Cyan polygons or polylines

Semi-transparent yellow masked reference geometry required by the operation, such as:

* revolve axis;
* sweep path;
* chamfer/fillet edges/faces;
* loft section faces.

Use cyan_polylines for line-like geometry.
Use cyan_polygons for area-like reference regions.

### 3. Red polylines

Red curves represent the driving 2D sketch or profile used by the next operation.

For an additive or subtractive profile operation, the red profile should
normally form one or more valid closed sketch loops.

Do not draw current-model edges in red unless they are explicitly part
of the new driving sketch.

### 4. Blue polylines

Blue curves represent the termination contour or final section contour
of the local feature generated by the operation.

Examples include:

* the opposite contour of an extrusion;
* the final section of a sweep;
* the terminal contour of a partial revolve;
* the terminating profile of a loft.

### 5. Green polylines

Green curves represent other forming edges of the local feature,
including edges connecting the red profile to the blue termination
contour.

## Polyline representation rules

1. Every independent visible curve must be a separate polyline object.
2. Do not combine disconnected curves into one points array.
3. Do not combine two intersecting but topologically independent edges
   into one polyline.
4. Preserve whether each polyline is open or closed.
5. Do not repeat the first point at the end of a closed polyline.
6. For a clearly straight segment, use exactly its two endpoints.
7. For a curved segment, use enough approximately arc-length-uniform
   points to reproduce its shape accurately.
8. Normally use between 8 and 32 points for one curved segment.
9. Use more points only when necessary for a complex projected curve.
10. Preserve visible perspective deformation, including circles that
    appear as ellipses.
11. Do not replace projected curves with idealized front-view circles.
12. Do not output hidden or occluded edges unless they are explicitly
    part of the pseudo-preview convention.
13. All coordinates must lie inside [0, 1].
14. Use at least four decimal places when practical.

## Mask polygon rules

1. Each disconnected mask region must be represented separately.
2. Polygon vertices must follow the visible boundary in order.
3. Set closed to true.
4. Do not repeat the first point at the end.
5. Do not merge separate regions.
6. Do not include the grayscale model background inside a mask unless
   that area is genuinely part of the reference-plane overlay.

### Mandatory reference-selection procedure

Before predicting the new feature, internally perform the following
steps in order:

1. Inspect `current_state` only and identify candidate existing
   topology represented by the magenta wireframe.

2. Identify candidate existing faces whose boundaries are
   represented by consistent magenta edge loops.

3. Identify candidate existing edges represented by individual magenta
   curves or continuous magenta edge chains.

4. Select only the reference geometry required by the provided
   operation_type.

5. Verify that every selected existing face, edge can be
   traced back to `current_state`.

6. Only after the reference geometry has been validated, infer the red,
   blue, and green geometry of the new feature.

## One-step modeling constraint

Infer exactly ONE CAD feature.

The predicted feature must:

1. use exactly the provided operation_type;
2. be executable as one CAD modeling operation;
3. modify only one local feature region;
4. move the current model closer to the final model;
5. not attempt to construct all missing final geometry at once;
6. not combine multiple operation types;
7. not include a fillet or chamfer together with an extrusion, revolve,
sweep, loft, or other feature;
8. use the smallest simple feature that reasonably explains one visible
difference between the current and final states.

Multiple valid construction sequences may exist. Select one simple,
locally connected, geometrically plausible next step.

## Operation-specific geometric guidance

For extrude_add, extrude_cut, symmetric_extrude_add, or
symmetric_extrude_cut:

* red: driving sketch profile;
* blue: extrusion termination contour;
* green: corresponding side or forming edges;
* yellow: sketch support plane or face;
* cyan: normally empty unless an explicit reference is required.

For revolve_add, revolve_cut, symmetric_revolve_add, or
symmetric_revolve_cut:

* red: driving profile;
* cyan: revolve axis;
* blue: terminal contour when visibly applicable;
* green: other visible edges formed by the revolution;
* yellow: sketch support plane or face.

For sweep_add or sweep_cut:

* red: driving section profile;
* cyan: sweep path;
* blue: terminal section;
* green: visible forming edges connecting sections;
* yellow: sketch support plane or face when visible.

For loft_add or loft_cut:

* red: starting or driving section;
* blue: terminating section;
* green: visible correspondence or forming edges;
* yellow: relevant section support planes when visible.

For fillet or chamfer:

* represent only the local preview edges and affected reference
  geometry that are visually meaningful;
* do not invent a new sketch profile unless the representation
  convention explicitly requires one.

For cplane:

* output the yellow reference-plane polygon;
* red, blue, and green layers should normally be empty.
